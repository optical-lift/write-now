## The major correction to the previous checkpoint

The earlier blind canon adjudication had **14 direct structural matches**.

Under the stricter law-identity language, only **3 of those 14** survive as `SAME_LAW`.

Three additional `SAME_LAW` results come from pairs previously labeled only partial.

This demonstrates why “direct structural match” and “same law” had to remain separate concepts.

Most old direct matches shared a dependency template but differed in a necessary condition.

Examples:

- Mark: operator identity contributes beyond state.
- Canon: actor identity matters only as **source-assigned jurisdiction**.

Those are not the same law under the strict condition rule. The canon side has an additional necessary relation that Mark did not measure.

The correct relation is:

`DISTINCT_LAW_SHARED_PATTERN`

This operationalizes the user's earlier correction that the same visible or general pattern under genuinely different relevant conditions can represent different laws.

## Same-law equivalence cluster EQC-001

### Neutral description

> **Component inventory cannot substitute for relational placement/order.**

Canon anchor:

`CIR-018 — Ordered physical arrangement is functionally significant`

Five independent Mark constraints satisfy the same-law test:

- `MCR-016` — the context-feature inventory cannot replace actual relational placement;
- `MCR-034` — preserving observation identities while destroying spatial placement destroys substantial state organization;
- `MCR-083` — preserving identities/statistics while destroying spatial topology collapses recurrence;
- `MCR-087` — cross-source recurrence exceeds null worlds that destroy spatial/structural organization;
- `MCR-007` — holding the same members fixed while reversing/shuffling order destroys path information.

Across these experiments and the canon rule, the same relational dependency survives normalization:

```text
same constituents / identities
        +
relation or order among them
        ↓
organized consequence / function

remove or scramble the relation
        ↓
the prior consequence structure cannot remain intact
```

The surface domains differ:

- sequence;
- local context;
- spatial state field;
- topology;
- cross-source recurrence;
- sacrificial physical arrangement.

Those differences do not add a new necessary condition to the normalized law.

This is therefore the strongest current same-law equivalence in the project.

### Why this is stronger than analogy

The experiments do not merely say “relationships matter.”

They repeatedly perform the decisive contrast:

> preserve the things; alter the relation.

The consequence changes.

That is the same dependency topology.

## Seventeen different laws sharing deeper patterns

The largest class is not failure.

It is exactly the structure the user anticipated:

> similar dependency shape, but a genuinely different relevant condition makes it a different law.

Important recurring shared patterns include:

### State + participant

Several Mark experiments show state alone is insufficient and participant/operator identity adds information.

Canon shows a superficially similar structure, but the operative participant condition is **assigned jurisdiction/authorization**.

Because jurisdiction is independently necessary, the laws remain different.

### Ordered history

Mark transition/history/composition experiments and canon counted/state-chain rules both say that prior sequence matters.

But the specific required histories differ:

- generic recent structural state;
- counted interval from embodied event;
- authorization followed by non-success;
- long organic/referent state chain.

These are related constraint families, not one law.

### Context-conditioned operation

Mark context changes consequence.

Canon governing context changes permitted operation.

But canon's condition includes source-defined state and jurisdiction that Mark has not independently recovered.

Again: shared pattern, different law.

This is the empirical equivalent of the user's “11 different conditions can mean 11 laws” rule.

## What is now actually established

At this checkpoint, the project has one strongly replicated cross-lane equivalence:

> **The identity/inventory of constituents is insufficient to preserve function or consequence when the relevant relation/order among those constituents is changed.**

This equivalence was recovered through five independent Mark constraints and one independently frozen canon rule.

A second, less replicated equivalence is:

> **Current state identity constrains the consequence contract even inside a shared larger container.**

Neither is yet called a universal law.

## What remains unproven

This checkpoint does not establish:

- that EQC-001 or EQC-002 apply to all substrates;
- that they are irreducible fundamental laws;
- that the canon is a complete inventory of such laws;
- that created physical systems outside Mark/canon obey them;
- that the same-law clusters imply common authorship or historical transmission;
- that the constraint is metaphysically necessary rather than repeatedly instantiated.

Those require independent created-world lanes and held-out prediction.
