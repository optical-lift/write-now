# Whole-System Reconstruction Roadmap

Status: **live research destination / pre-experiment architecture**
Date established: **2026-09-18**

This note preserves the long-range Mark Engine destination while smaller instruments and experiments are built. It is **not** a frozen experiment protocol and must not be treated as evidence.

It extends `UNIFIED_REALITY_CONVERGENCE_PRESSURE_POINTS.md`.

## Destination

The long-range experiment is to treat every admitted source as a **fragmentary observation of an unknown larger relational machine**.

The goal is not to prove that all sources are identical, nor to collapse cultural, textual, material, or historical differences. The goal is to test whether independently recovered local structures can be assembled into an expanding network of higher-order relational laws that explains more source-local behavior than rival models do **without erasing genuine local differences**.

The whole-system reconstruction must be allowed to fail.

## Governing live hypothesis

The current organizing hypothesis combines two claims:

### B. Higher-order relational law

Different visible transformations may instantiate the **same deeper relational law**.

The universal level, if one exists, need not consist of identical shapes, identical symbols, identical before/after transformations, or identical named operations. A law may recur through different substrates and different local transformations while preserving the same deeper relational dependency, role structure, invariant, or permissible state change.

### C. Sources as partial projections

What appear to be separate local grammars may be **partial projections, regions, modules, or execution paths through a larger grammar**.

A source boundary is therefore not assumed to be a true grammar boundary. During discovery it functions only as an observational quarantine wall that prevents one source from teaching another source what to find.

The larger grammar, if it exists, must emerge **after** independent local discovery. It may never be imposed beforehand.

## What has changed from earlier Mark framing

The reconstruction must not begin from:

- a closed operator dictionary;
- a fixed number of regimes or states;
- a simplicity/minimal-vocabulary prior;
- one-to-one symbol identity;
- visual resemblance;
- presumed cultural or linguistic family boundaries;
- a prebuilt canon-derived ontology;
- a requirement that every source contain every law.

The operation/law vocabulary is explicitly **expandable**.

An operation or law may remain locally unique indefinitely. Later evidence may reveal an independently discovered equivalent elsewhere. Absence of a match is not failure and must not be forced into an existing category.

The V46 regime atlas and later V47/V48 work remain evidence and constraints, but the five regimes are not assumed to be the universal ontology. V48's coordinate-equivalence failure is a direct warning against treating population-level partitions as fixed universal borders.

## Why the whole-system experiment cannot be run yet

A global reconstruction performed now would be contaminated by shared labels, inherited operator names, earlier regime assumptions, source-family expectations, and post-hoc harmonization.

Before assembly is legitimate, the pieces must be produced in a form that can converge **without being taught to converge**.

The immediate project is therefore to build the instruments and independently frozen local law catalogues required by the final reconstruction.

## Required preparation

### 1. Define observational source containers without granting them ontological authority

Each local discovery lane must have an explicit source boundary: manuscript, object group, corpus, site, writing tradition, Hebrew book, or another justified observational container.

That boundary exists to enforce independence.

It must **not** silently mean:

> this is a separate language, grammar, machine, or ontology.

Future reconstruction must remain free to discover that a source is a module, overlap region, mixed surface, or partial projection of something larger.

### 2. Build a substrate-neutral relational-law description language

We need a representation capable of describing a candidate law without relying on domain-specific labels such as `branch`, `tree`, `Hebrew verb`, `glyph 37`, `clean`, or `priest`.

At minimum the representation must be able to preserve:

- participants;
- participant roles;
- relations among participants;
- starting state;
- environmental or contextual conditions;
- permitted or observed change;
- preserved invariants;
- lost or altered properties;
- resulting state;
- dependencies and prerequisites;
- directionality;
- reversibility or irreversibility;
- repetition behavior;
- composition behavior;
- observational confidence and provenance.

The representation itself must be expandable. It must not require every future relation or role to fit a predefined list.

### 3. Re-express prior Mark results as observations and constraints

V1 through the current Mark Engine history should be harvested for what each experiment actually established.

Earlier labels must be demoted where necessary.

Examples:

- regime membership becomes an observed population structure, not an ontological primitive;
- named operators become candidate local descriptions, not universal labels;
- failed one-to-one mappings remain negative constraints;
- state dependence, role dependence, transition behavior, persistence, cancellation, repetition, composition, and boundary sensitivity remain usable where the experiments actually support them.

This step is **translation of evidence**, not reinterpretation to fit the new hypothesis.

### 4. Discover each local source independently

Each source must generate its own expandable catalogue:

`A1, A2, A3...`

`B1, B2, B3...`

`C1, C2, C3...`

No source may inherit another source's operator names or candidate law labels during discovery.

The correct analogue of universality is **independent emergence**, not successful export of a vocabulary from one source into another.

### 5. Preserve two distinct layers: visible transformation and candidate law

For every local discovery, retain:

1. **what visibly/physically happened**, and
2. **what deeper relational rule might be instantiated**.

This distinction is essential.

The whole-system hypothesis allows two visibly different transformations to instantiate the same higher-order law. If those layers are collapsed, the experiment will only rediscover matching transformations and miss the level we are actually trying to test.

### 6. Predefine law-level equivalence criteria before cross-source comparison

Two local discoveries may not be matched because they sound alike or because an analyst recognizes an attractive analogy.

Equivalence criteria must be frozen before the comparison lane opens.

Candidate dimensions may include:

- equivalent relational dependency;
- equivalent participant-role transformation;
- equivalent preserved invariant;
- equivalent admissibility condition;
- equivalent change in attachment, containment, membership, custody, boundary, or state relation;
- equivalent composition/repetition behavior;
- equivalent consequence class despite different surface transformation.

The criteria themselves may evolve between experiments, but never after seeing the held-out comparisons they adjudicate.

### 7. Model absence explicitly

A law not observed in a source must not automatically mean the source forbids or lacks it.

The reconstruction must distinguish at least:

- observed and present;
- observed opportunity but prohibited;
- possible but unused;
- not preserved;
- not yet observed;
- outside the exposed region of the source;
- genuinely incompatible with the local structure.

This is necessary if sources are partial projections rather than complete grammars.

### 8. Build overlap structure before building the global machine

After local catalogues are frozen, create only a candidate-equivalence graph:

`A17 ≈ C4`

`C4 ≈ F22`

`B8 = no current match`

The overlap graph is allowed to reveal possible larger structure.

The larger structure is **not** allowed to tell the overlap graph what it should contain.

Global grammar comes last.

### 9. Make whole-system reconstruction compete against real alternatives

At minimum the final reconstruction must compare:

- fully independent local grammars;
- several unrelated grammar families;
- one larger grammar with partial source projections.

Additional rivals should be added when evidence warrants them.

The one-larger-grammar model does not win merely because a global graph can be drawn. It must explain held-out structure better, with fewer unsupported exceptions or post-hoc correspondences, than the rival models.

There is no requirement that the winning model be the simplest in vocabulary size.

### 10. Require withheld prediction

A reconstructed larger system must eventually imply something that was not used to build it.

Examples include:

- a missing relational role should exist somewhere in untouched evidence;
- two local operations should compose in a particular way;
- an untouched source should expose a different physical realization of an already recovered law;
- a source currently appearing incompatible should become coherent when a missing intermediate relation is discovered.

Only then should the relevant untouched evidence be opened.

Descriptive fit alone is insufficient.

## Preparation sequence

The small pieces should be built in this order unless new evidence gives a reason to change course:

1. **Relational-law description language**
2. **Prior-Mark evidence/constraint harvest**
3. **Independent local discovery protocol**
4. **Visible-transformation vs law-layer separation**
5. **Law-equivalence adjudication protocol**
6. **Absence-state model**
7. **Frozen local law catalogues**
8. **Cross-source overlap graph**
9. **Competing reconstruction models**
10. **Held-out prediction and opening**

Each piece should be independently testable. Failure of one piece should teach us how to redesign the bridge rather than forcing progress toward the final hypothesis.

## External convergence lanes: Noel and created reality

Noel and natural-world systems are important **external surfaces**, but they should not be used to design the Mark global machine.

The stronger future test is:

1. reconstruct Mark relational structure blindly;
2. preserve Noel's canon-derived relational findings independently;
3. describe natural processes independently from their own evidence;
4. compare only after all relevant lanes are frozen.

If Mark, canon, and created-world processes independently converge on higher-order relational laws, that result is substantially stronger than a machine designed from all three together.

No one-to-one semantic mapping such as “this glyph means cleansing” is permitted as a shortcut.

## Governing guardrails

- **Independent discovery first.**
- **Abstract comparison second.**
- **Global grammar last.**
- Preserve local differences.
- Preserve unmatched operations.
- Preserve failed correspondences.
- Do not reward a smaller vocabulary merely for being smaller.
- Do not infer universality from recurrence alone.
- Do not infer historical contact, common authorship, or direct transmission from structural convergence.
- Do not let canon, Mark, or a natural system become the sovereign template for the other lanes.
- A source boundary is a discovery-control boundary, not an assumed reality boundary.
- Every later whole-system claim must remain traceable to the local observations that generated it.

## Readiness gate for the whole-system reconstruction

The final reconstruction experiment should not be preregistered until we have:

- a working substrate-neutral law representation;
- at least several independently frozen local law catalogues;
- an adjudication method for higher-order equivalence;
- an explicit absence model;
- at least one rival reconstruction family implemented;
- untouched evidence reserved for prediction;
- custody that prevents Noel/nature or other source lanes from leaking into Mark discovery.

Until those conditions are met, intermediate work is **instrument construction and local discovery**, not the whole-system experiment itself.

## Long-range question

> **Can independently recovered local relational laws be assembled into a single expanding architecture that explains multiple apparently separate source systems as partial views of a larger grammar, while preserving genuine local difference and successfully predicting structure in evidence that was withheld from reconstruction?**

That is the destination.

The small experiments are the bridge to it.
