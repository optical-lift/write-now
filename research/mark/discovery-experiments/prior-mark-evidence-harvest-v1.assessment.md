## What the accumulated evidence constrains

The following are harvest-level constraints. They are not universal-law assignments.

### 1. The recurring structure is real, but a closed vocabulary is repeatedly unsafe

The earliest blind worlds and the real-corpus challenge found cross-source recurrence above null worlds.

At the same time:

- unseen observations often require abstention rather than forced assignment;
- only part of the real corpus falls inside already learned family envelopes;
- the tested finite glyph functional quotient loses much of the operator-specific information;
- exact identity sometimes preserves information that coarse physical classes lose;
- V48 shows that even a stable five-region atlas should not be assumed to have fixed independently rebuildable borders.

The evidence therefore constrains the next stage against assuming a small, closed, globally fixed operator dictionary.

### 2. State is recurrent, spatially organized, mixed, and history-sensitive

The blind state work supports:

- recurrent local states;
- strong spatial organization of those states;
- source-local mixtures rather than one state per source;
- nonrandom state transitions and short programs;
- strong commitment/return asymmetry;
- a small but transferable second-order memory effect.

This is incompatible with treating observations as independent static labels.

### 3. Operator identity matters beyond state, but the operative identity is not yet known

Several experiments show that current operator identity adds consequence information beyond incoming state.

Counterfactual glyph substitution strengthens that observation: holding the incoming state fixed while replacing the operator changes consequence prediction.

But:

- exact conventional identity may proxy unmeasured physical or relational structure;
- coarse finite operator classes lose signal;
- anonymous structural-role factorization does not yet replace operator identity.

So the evidence supports operator-specific consequence without identifying the final operator basis.

### 4. Composition exists, but not as one simple complete algebra

The composition line contains both strong positives and important limits.

Supported:

- independent one-step kernels can compose into useful two-step predictions;
- supported repetition/idempotence subsets transfer;
- supported ordered cancellation subsets transfer.

Constraints:

- V12 composition did not transfer consistently across both lanes;
- V13 found no matched direct-pair residual;
- V45 later retained a small direct-pair residual over factorized composition.

These results are not contradictions once their experimental definitions are preserved.

They constrain the future grammar to allow:

- substantial factorization;
- special composition families;
- possible residual higher-order interactions;
- representation- and support-dependent composition behavior.

### 5. Hierarchical/topological structure repeatedly survives, but current relation labels are not yet privileged laws

Positive evidence includes:

- topology-edit atoms;
- literal vertex-edit constraints;
- selected edge-edit constraints;
- deeper local wiring motifs;
- hierarchical compression;
- typed critical structure outperforming residual geometry.

But matched interventions did **not** independently distinguish:

- generic protected relationships;
- literal relationship rewiring;
- typed relationship classes;
- recursive composition protection.

This means the data support structured topology/hierarchy while simultaneously warning against declaring the current relation labels to be the true law vocabulary.

### 6. Geometry is secondary in some tests, not absent

Several results reduce geometry after structural matching:

- the V5 edge-turn counter-signal collapses after matched structure;
- topology-only hierarchical compression still adjudicates strongly.

But V35-P and V36 also show that computer-readable physical form can carry cross-modal structural information.

The constraint is therefore not “geometry does not matter.”

It is:

> geometry must be separated from topology, identity, sequence, and relational role rather than treated as the default explanatory layer.

### 7. Hebrew/glyph evidence argues strongly against fixed identity mapping

The cross-system sequence is especially important.

The record contains:

1. a V15 fingerprint correspondence under its original design;
2. V16 failure under stronger stress controls;
3. V18 localization of one sensitivity to proper-noun exclusion;
4. V19 failure of common fixed pairings;
5. glyph-side context/transition effects in V20/V23;
6. Hebrew operator main effects but no mirrored interaction profile;
7. failure of recurrence-consequence and first-passage interaction bridges;
8. V35-P directional transition information from glyph form;
9. V36 order-sensitive hidden-path information, with exact identity stronger than coarse form and morphology stronger overall than lemma.

Taken together, the evidence does **not** support “glyph X equals Hebrew token Y.”

It does support continuing to test whether different substrates independently expose structurally comparable operations or constraints at a higher abstraction.

That comparison has not yet been performed in this harvest.

### 8. Documentary boundaries should not be promoted to process boundaries

The first-passage work exposed heavy boundary censoring.

V25 and V26 then failed to find a special structural continuation signal across verse/book-block boundaries under their tested representations.

This constrains the next stage in both directions:

- do not assume documentary boundaries are the underlying process;
- do not assume hidden continuity merely because the boundary is documentary.

Boundary behavior remains unresolved at the deeper-law level.

### 9. Some apparent temporal structure is not operator-specific

V29 recovered a narrow proximal full-morph footprint around a masked operator.

V30 then failed to show that the footprint changes selectively when the operator identity is counterfactually replaced.

Therefore the temporal association should be preserved without upgrading it into an operator-specific causal footprint.

### 10. A transferable atlas and unstable rebuilt borders can both be true

V46 shows that a fixed five-region structural atlas transfers strongly to unseen sources.

V47 shows regime-dependent scale persistence and no additional discrete longer-history program layer.

V48 shows that rebuilding an equivalent-shaped five-cluster coordinate system on a smaller source population moves the borders too far to count as the same coordinate system.

The combined constraint is:

> stable organization exists, but the partition itself should not be assumed to be the ontology.

This is one of the strongest reasons the next stage should compare independently discovered relational laws rather than compare regime labels.

### 11. Calibration success is not universality evidence

The white-paint line is a useful methodological control.

The planted grammar is recovered perfectly in calibration.

Transfer is then mixed:

- some repetition/degree and closure-phase behavior transfers to glyph proxies;
- physical witnesses retain some repetition-degree structure;
- other predicted dimensions fail;
- appearance-system classification can outperform the white-paint grammar.

This demonstrates why a clean synthetic grammar should be treated as an instrument calibration, not as evidence that reality has adopted the calibration vocabulary.

## Next stage

The next checkpoint should **not** build the global grammar yet.

It should design the **law-equivalence adjudication language**:

> Given two independently discovered observation packets, under what frozen, substrate-neutral criteria are they allowed to count as the same relational law, a specialization of one law, an inverse, a composition, a contextual variant, or unrelated?

The equivalence criteria must be defined before candidate cross-source matches are opened.

That is where the “expandable opposite-of-4” direction becomes operational.
