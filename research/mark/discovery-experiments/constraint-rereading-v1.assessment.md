## Question

Re-read the 84 canonical active Mark evidence packets under the newly clarified hypothesis:

> **A law is not primarily an operation label. It is a constraint: given the complete relevant relational condition, some consequence cannot be freely substituted, discarded, collapsed, or remain unchanged without an observable consequence.**

The purpose of this checkpoint is to find where Mark had already demonstrated constraint-shaped structure without recognizing that as the primary object.

This checkpoint does **not** decide which constraints are the same law.

## The important surprise

The Mark record does **not** mainly contain simple “forbidden event never occurs” laws.

Only two eligible projections are directly exclusion-shaped.

Most of the law-shaped evidence has a deeper form:

> **You cannot remove, exchange, reorder, compress, or ignore part of the relevant relation and preserve the same consequence structure.**

That is substantially closer to the condition/consequence model than to an operator dictionary.

The prohibition is often exposed experimentally by trying to violate a relation while holding something else fixed.

## Strongest direct constraint demonstrations

These are not declared to be the same law. They are simply the clearest places where the experiment directly changes or destroys one part of the condition while preserving important controls.

### MCR-007 — order cannot be discarded

Source: `RLOP-007`, V36.

The same glyph members and candidate inventory are retained, but reversing or shuffling order materially degrades hidden-path recovery.

Constraint:

> With the same members present, **order cannot be removed while preserving the same consequence information**.

This is stronger than “sequence matters.” It says constituent identity is insufficient to define the relation.

### MCR-016 — placement cannot be replaced by inventory

Source: `RLOP-016`, V40.

The feature vocabulary and left context are retained while the right-context membership relation is deliberately broken. Every one of the 20 frozen nulls becomes worse.

Constraint:

> The things present cannot substitute for **where/how they stand in relation** to one another.

### MCR-021 — repetition need not create a new consequence

Source: `RLOP-021`, V45.

For a supported subset, A→A preserves the one-step consequence kernel within the frozen tolerance.

Constraint:

> A longer physical path cannot automatically be treated as a different consequence law.

This is direct evidence for the user's correction that physical step count is not fundamental.

### MCR-022 — a two-step path can be constrained toward the starting state

Source: `RLOP-022`, V45.

For 20 frozen A→B pairs, the composite consequence returns toward the incoming state; all 20 transfer in holdout, control and topology-only form.

Constraint:

> The visible sequence A→B cannot be interpreted merely as “two things happened.” Its ordered relation constrains the resultant state relative to the state before either operation.

### MCR-029 — the first prohibition-shaped blind rule

Source: `RLOP-029`, blind-discovery v1.

A frozen local relation suppresses a candidate consequence below matched-null expectation in both holdout and control.

Constraint:

> Under this relational condition, that consequence **cannot be treated as freely occurring at its ordinary/null rate**.

Important ceiling: this is statistical exclusion, not proof of absolute logical impossibility.

This finding was originally demoted because the old candidate system privileged positive lift. Under the constraint model, its negative sign is scientifically interesting rather than second-class.

### MCR-034 — spatial relation cannot be destroyed without consequence

Source: `RLOP-034`, local-state-field v1.

Observation identity remains, but spatial organization is destroyed. Same-state organization falls substantially.

Constraint:

> State organization cannot be specified independently of actual spatial relation.

### MCR-059 — state alone cannot determine consequence

Source: `RLOP-059`, V11.

Incoming state is held fixed while a supported alternative operator is substituted. The consequence prediction changes and the actual operator wins across holdout/control.

Constraint:

> **State alone is not the complete condition.**

Changing another participant in the condition changes consequence even when state is fixed.

### MCR-083 — identity/statistics cannot substitute for topology

Source: `RLOP-083`, V5 real-corpus challenge.

Observation identity, source grouping, grayscale histogram and measurement pipeline are retained. Spatial topology is destroyed. Cross-source recurrence collapses.

Constraint:

> The physical constituents and their marginal properties cannot preserve the recurring structure once the relation among them is destroyed.

This is one of the cleanest demonstrations that **relation is not decorative information added on top of objects**.

## What else the re-reading established

### Current state is repeatedly insufficient

Multiple experiments independently show that consequence cannot be represented adequately from current state alone.

The record contains additional information in:

- previous structural history;
- current operator identity;
- relational context;
- actual placement;
- sequence order;
- local topology;
- source-local transition dynamics.

This does not yet tell us which of those variables are fundamental.

It does tell us that a state-label-only ontology is too small.

### Object/participant inventory is repeatedly insufficient

The same members can behave differently when:

- their order changes;
- their local pairing changes;
- their spatial topology is destroyed;
- an operator is replaced while state is fixed.

The system therefore cannot be modeled as a bag of entities with fixed meanings.

### Physical path length is not law identity

The repetition and composition work demonstrates:

- one physical step and two repeated steps can preserve the same consequence kernel for supported cases;
- two distinct ordered steps can constrain the composite state back toward the incoming state;
- independently learned one-step consequence relations can produce the two-step consequence without a special semantic A+B label.

This is already incompatible with identifying a law by the number of visible operations used to express it.

### Closed vocabularies repeatedly fail

The re-reading retains several representation constraints:

- a finite operator quotient loses substantial consequence information;
- learned family systems require abstention for unfamiliar observations;
- fixed Hebrew↔glyph identity maps fail;
- gross structural twins are not automatically functional identities;
- five-regime borders depend on the fitted population;
- simple morphology, local relation categories and recurrence consequences do not recover the missing law.

These are not themselves universal laws.

They are warnings about how **not** to name the laws.

## Why negative experiments mattered

The earlier research program often asked:

> Does representation X predict outcome Y?

A failed prediction was therefore treated as a dead end.

Under the constraint reading, many failures have a different use:

> X **cannot be substituted for** the fuller condition without losing the consequence structure.

That is still only a representation constraint unless the experiment manipulates the underlying observed system.

The distinction prevents us from making the equal-and-opposite mistake of declaring every null model a cosmic prohibition.

## What Mark had already established before we named it

The strongest defensible synthesis of the eligible corpus is:

> **Consequence in the Mark systems is relationally conditioned. It is not fully determined by isolated state, isolated object identity, unordered constituents, or a fixed finite label vocabulary. Changing relevant order, placement, topology, history, operator identity, or composition can change or constrain the consequence while other properties remain fixed.**

That statement is supported by the experimental record.

A stronger statement—

> these are manifestations of one or more universal constraints governing created reality—

has **not** yet been experimentally adjudicated.

That is the next problem.

## Relation to the newly clarified law hypothesis

The re-reading gives us a concrete experimental form for “cannot”:

```text
hold much of the condition fixed
        +
alter/remove one relational component
        ↓
the old consequence structure cannot remain unchanged
```

or, for invariance:

```text
change the visible physical path
        ↓
the consequence is constrained to remain the same / return toward a prior state
```

That means the future law-equivalence test should not compare nouns or operation names.

It should compare **constraint signatures**:

- what must be held;
- what may vary;
- what cannot be removed;
- what cannot coexist;
- what cannot follow;
- what remains invariant;
- what consequence appears when the relation is changed.
