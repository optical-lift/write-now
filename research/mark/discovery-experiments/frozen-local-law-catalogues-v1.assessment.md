## Purpose

This checkpoint asked whether the project had reached the point where independently frozen local law catalogues could be built without allowing a later global reconstruction to rewrite the pieces.

The answer is **partly yes**.

We now have frozen local inventories and explicit absence-state projections.

But the checkpoint also exposed a new prerequisite:

> the current primary Mark catalogues are frozen experiment-local evidence surfaces, not yet certified independent raw sources.

Therefore the project is **not yet ready for a cross-source overlap graph**.

## Critical readiness failure

All three primary Mark catalogues currently have:

`raw_source_independence = UNKNOWN_SHARED_SUBSTRATE_POSSIBLE`

and share the overlap group:

`MARK_HEBREW_GLYPH_SEQUENCE_FAMILY`

Therefore:

> **M001, M002 and M003 may not be counted as three independent source occurrences of a law.**

They are distinct frozen discovery surfaces, but their underlying substrate may overlap.

This distinction was not explicit enough in the original roadmap.

## Why this matters

If the project built the overlap graph now, it could make a false universality signal:

```text
experiment A found law L
experiment B found law L
experiment C found law L
therefore L independently recurs in three sources
```

when A, B and C may be re-measurements of overlapping evidence.

That would violate the entire independent-discovery premise.

## Multi-source historical evidence

Many strong Mark results come from multi-source blind experiments.

Those results remain valid support for law signatures.

But v1 does **not** assign them as local law presence to individual sources because the historical frozen packets do not yet provide source-level adjudications at the required law signature.

The rule is:

> multi-source transfer can support a law; it cannot manufacture local source occurrences.

## Checkpoint verdict

The Frozen Local Law Catalogue instrument works.

It successfully preserves:

- local discovery identity;
- source-boundary custody;
- external-validation separation;
- absence-state semantics;
- shared-law projection without rewriting local inventory.

But the **catalogue dataset is not yet sufficient for Step 8** of the original roadmap.

The correct next move is not the overlap graph.

## New prerequisite

### Source-Level Disaggregation and Independence Certification

Before the cross-source overlap graph, the Mark lane must recover or rebuild source-level catalogue entries from the frozen evidence.

At minimum:

1. identify exact raw source IDs/objects/books behind each eligible Mark law experiment;
2. determine which catalogues share underlying evidence;
3. where the historical design permits it, project law presence/opportunity separately by raw source;
4. use the frozen Absence-State Model for every evaluated source×law cell;
5. certify non-overlapping source containers before counting recurrence;
6. retain multi-source-only laws as global-support evidence when source localization is impossible.

This is a methodological correction produced by the experiment itself, not a failure.

## Readiness criterion for the overlap graph

Do not start the primary Mark overlap graph until there are at least several primary catalogues whose raw-source independence is either:

- `CERTIFIED_INDEPENDENT`, or
- explicitly modeled with non-overlap weights/constraints that prevent pseudo-replication.

The canon lane remains external and cannot satisfy this Mark readiness gate.
